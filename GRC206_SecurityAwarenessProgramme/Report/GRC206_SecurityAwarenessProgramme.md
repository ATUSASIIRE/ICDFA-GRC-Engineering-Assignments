## Executive Summary

Northbridge Logistics is a fictional logistics organisation with 800 employees across its head office, warehouses, and remote sales teams. Recent invoice-fraud attempts, employee password reuse following a breach, and the organisation's responsibility for regulated customer information demonstrate a need for an organised security awareness programme.

This report presents a practical 12-month programme based on five stages: **Assess, Plan, Deliver, Measure, and Improve**. It prioritises phishing and invoice fraud, weak password practices, and improper handling of sensitive information. The programme combines targeted training, employee onboarding, quarterly phishing simulations, accessible incident reporting, measurable performance indicators, and continuous improvement.

The proposed objectives are to:

- Reduce simulated-phishing click rates by at least **50%** from the Month 1 baseline by Month 12.
- Achieve at least **95% MFA adoption** among eligible employees by Month 6.
- Increase approved reporting of suspicious emails and potential security incidents by at least **40%** from baseline by Month 12.

These are planning targets and should be reviewed after the baseline assessment.

## 1. Introduction

Employees play a significant role in protecting organisational systems, information, and operations. Technical controls alone cannot prevent security incidents when users fail to verify payment requests, reuse credentials, mishandle sensitive information, or delay reporting suspicious activity.

The proposed programme is designed for a distributed workforce and a limited training budget. It assumes that Northbridge Logistics has corporate email accounts, internet access, shared digital storage, an established HR function, and a small security team.

### 1.1 Programme Aim

To reduce human-related cybersecurity risks, encourage safer employee behaviour, improve incident reporting, and strengthen the organisation's security culture over 12 months.

### 1.2 Programme Lifecycle

1. **Assess** human-driven risks and establish a baseline.
2. **Plan** objectives, responsibilities, and role-specific content.
3. **Deliver** accessible training and awareness activities.
4. **Measure** participation, knowledge, and security behaviour.
5. **Improve and Sustain** the programme through reviews, feedback, and updates.

## 2. Assess

### 2.1 Priority Human-Driven Risks

#### Risk 1: Phishing and Invoice Fraud

Attackers may impersonate suppliers, customers, or managers to request payment, obtain credentials, or persuade employees to open malicious links and attachments.

**Potential impact:**

- Financial loss
- Credential theft
- Unauthorised system access
- Operational disruption
- Reputational damage

**Required employee behaviour:**

- Verify payment instructions through a separate trusted channel.
- Inspect sender addresses, links, attachments, and unusual urgency.
- Escalate unexpected changes to supplier bank details.
- Report suspicious messages before acting.

#### Risk 2: Weak Password Practices and Password Reuse

Password reuse enables attackers to use credentials exposed in one breach to access additional accounts.

**Potential impact:**

- Account compromise
- Unauthorised access
- Exposure of customer information
- Lateral movement across company systems

**Required employee behaviour:**

- Use a unique password for every account.
- Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
- Never share login credentials.
- Follow approved password and access-control requirements.

#### Risk 3: Improper Handling of Sensitive Information

Employees may expose regulated information through insecure file sharing, unauthorised access, misdirected communication, or careless handling of physical records.

**Potential impact:**

- Privacy breaches
- Regulatory consequences
- Customer harm
- Reputational damage

**Required employee behaviour:**

- Use approved systems for storing and transmitting information.
- Share sensitive information only with authorised recipients.
- Follow data-protection and records-handling procedures.
- Report accidental disclosure immediately.

### 2.2 Audience Segmentation

| Audience | Primary Awareness Needs |
|---|---|
| Head Office Staff | Phishing detection, invoice verification, password security, and customer-data protection |
| Warehouse Staff | Secure device use, physical security, suspicious-activity reporting, and protection of delivery records |
| Remote Sales Staff | Secure remote access, MFA, phishing prevention, safe internet use, and secure customer-information handling |
| Managers and Leadership | Security governance, fraud prevention, escalation, accountability, and data-protection responsibilities |

### 2.3 Month 1 Baseline Assessment

The baseline will establish the organisation's starting position and allow later results to be evaluated objectively.

| Baseline Measure | Method | Evidence Source |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing susceptibility | Controlled simulated-phishing exercise | Simulation report |
| Security knowledge | Short employee questionnaire | Assessment results |
| Incident reporting | Review historical reports and reporting channels | Security incident logs |
| Password and MFA practices | Approved compliance review without collecting passwords | Identity and access-management reports |
| Previous participation | Review security-awareness training history | HR training records |

## 4. Deliver

### 4.1 Delivery Methods

The programme will use multiple low-cost formats to reach personnel across different locations and shifts:

1. **Short awareness sessions:** Monthly 15 to 30-minute sessions delivered in person or online.
2. **Email campaigns:** Concise security tips, examples, and reminders.
3. **Practical demonstrations:** Exercises on identifying phishing, validating invoices, and reporting incidents.
4. **Posters and visual materials:** Reminders placed in offices, warehouses, and shared spaces.
5. **Online learning:** Short videos, quizzes, and digital materials for distributed employees.

### 4.2 New-Employee Onboarding

All new employees should complete initial security-awareness training within their first **seven working days**. The onboarding module will cover:

- Password creation and protection
- Phishing and social engineering
- Customer-information protection
- Acceptable use of company systems and devices
- Security incident-reporting procedures

HR will maintain completion records and coordinate compliance with the Security Manager.

### 4.3 Quarterly Phishing Simulations

Controlled simulations will reflect realistic business communication, including:

- Supplier invoices
- Password-reset notifications
- Delivery updates
- Requests to change payment details

The simulations will measure message opening, link clicking, information submission, and correct reporting. Employees who interact with a simulation will receive immediate, supportive education. Results will be used for improvement rather than punishment or embarrassment.

### 4.4 Incident Reporting

Northbridge Logistics will establish a confidential and no-blame reporting process using a dedicated email address or approved reporting button.

Employees will be encouraged to report:

- Suspicious emails and invoice requests
- Unexpected password-reset notifications
- Lost or stolen devices
- Accidental disclosure of customer information
- Suspected unauthorised access

Prompt reporting should be recognised as responsible behaviour, including when an employee has clicked a suspicious link or disclosed information by mistake.

## 5. Measure

### 5.1 Performance Metrics

| Metric | Calculation | Data Source | Owner | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Training completion rate | Employees completing training / employees assigned x 100 | HR records | HR Manager | Monthly |
| Phishing click rate | Employees clicking simulated links / recipients x 100 | Simulation reports | Security Manager | Quarterly |
| MFA adoption rate | Eligible employees with MFA / total eligible employees x 100 | Identity reports | IT Support | Monthly |
| Incident reporting trend | Reporting volume compared with baseline | Incident logs | Security Manager | Monthly |
| Security knowledge score | Correct answers / total questions x 100 | Assessments | HR and Security Manager | Quarterly |

### 5.2 Interpretation

- Lower phishing click rates indicate improved threat recognition.
- Higher MFA adoption indicates stronger account protection.
- Higher reporting volumes may indicate better awareness and trust in the reporting process, not necessarily more incidents.
- Completion rates indicate programme reach but do not independently prove behavioural improvement.
- Higher knowledge scores indicate improved understanding but should be considered alongside observed behaviour.

### 5.3 Management Reporting

The Security Manager will prepare monthly summaries and quarterly management reports covering:

- Training participation and completion
- Phishing simulation results
- MFA adoption
- Incident and reporting trends
- Employee feedback
- Identified weaknesses
- Corrective actions and resource needs

Reports will compare current results with the Month 1 baseline and approved objectives. Limitations such as incomplete reporting, role changes, access differences, and limited security-team capacity will be documented.

## 6. Improve and Sustain

### 6.1 Quarterly Reviews

The quarterly review team will include representatives from Executive Management, Security, HR, IT Support, and department management. It will examine:

- Simulation results
- Incident reports
- Training completion
- Employee feedback
- Changes in threats
- Progress against corrective actions

Actions will be assigned to named owners, given target dates, and tracked to completion.

### 6.2 Content Updates

Training materials will be updated when:

- New phishing or invoice-fraud techniques are identified.
- An incident reveals a knowledge gap.
- New systems or technologies are introduced.
- Data-protection obligations or internal procedures change.
- Employee feedback identifies accessibility or comprehension issues.

For example, if warehouse staff struggle to identify false delivery notifications, the next module should include realistic warehouse-specific examples.

### 6.3 Employee Feedback

Feedback will be collected through:

- Short online questionnaires
- Anonymous feedback forms
- Department meetings
- Warehouse-supervisor discussions
- Remote-sales feedback sessions

The programme will accommodate different roles, shifts, work locations, and levels of technical knowledge.

### 6.4 Employee Recognition

Two recognition methods will reinforce positive behaviour:

1. **Security Awareness Recognition:** Certificates or departmental acknowledgement for consistent participation and good practice.
2. **Responsible Reporting Recognition:** Positive acknowledgement for reporting suspicious activity or potential incidents.

Recognition will support learning and responsible behaviour without exposing employees who make honest mistakes.

### 6.5 Sustainability

Security awareness will be embedded into normal operations through:

- Monthly awareness messages
- Quarterly phishing simulations
- New-employee onboarding
- Annual refresher training
- Continuous MFA monitoring
- Regular content updates
- Quarterly governance reviews

This approach allows the programme to continue despite a small security team and limited budget.

## 7. Conclusion

The proposed Security Awareness Programme gives Northbridge Logistics a structured method for addressing human-driven cybersecurity risk. It connects three priority risks to targeted training, practical reporting procedures, measurable objectives, and continuous improvement.

The programme is designed to be achievable for a distributed organisation with limited resources. Leadership sponsorship, role-specific content, supportive phishing simulations, strong onboarding, and behavioural metrics will help Northbridge Logistics build a sustainable security culture and reduce avoidable incidents over the 12-month period.

---

## 8. Personal Reflection

Designing the programme required balancing broad security-awareness coverage against the constraints of 800 employees, multiple work environments, a small security team, and a limited budget. Short sessions, email campaigns, practical demonstrations, and quarterly reviews were selected to provide meaningful coverage without requiring expensive delivery methods or excessive disruption.

A second challenge was selecting measures that go beyond attendance. Phishing simulation results, MFA adoption, incident reporting, and knowledge scores provide a stronger view of behaviour and programme effectiveness than completion rates alone.

The assignment demonstrates that cybersecurity depends not only on technical controls but also on awareness, accountability, reporting confidence, leadership support, and continual improvement.
