## Project Overview

This repository presents a complete **12-month Security Awareness Programme** for **Northbridge Logistics**, a fictional logistics company with 800 employees working across its head office, warehouses, and remote sales teams.

The programme responds to three priority human-driven cybersecurity risks:

- Phishing and invoice fraud
- Weak password practices and password reuse
- Improper handling of sensitive and regulated customer information

It follows a five-stage lifecycle:

1. **Assess** existing risks and employee behaviour.
2. **Plan** measurable objectives, responsibilities, and audience-specific training.
3. **Deliver** practical awareness activities across different work environments.
4. **Measure** behavioural and programme-performance outcomes.
5. **Improve and Sustain** the programme through reviews, feedback, recognition, and continuous updates.

## Business Scenario

Northbridge Logistics has recently experienced invoice-fraud attempts, and two employees reused passwords after a security breach. The company also handles regulated customer information. These conditions create financial, operational, privacy, and reputational risks that require a structured security awareness programme.

The proposed approach assumes that the organisation has corporate email, internet access, shared digital storage, an HR function, a small security team, and a limited training budget.

## Priority Risks and Expected Behaviours

### 1. Phishing and Invoice Fraud

Employees may receive fraudulent messages impersonating suppliers, customers, or company leadership.

**Expected behaviour:**

- Verify payment instructions through an independent, trusted channel.
- Inspect messages for suspicious links, attachments, sender addresses, and urgency cues.
- Report unusual payment or account-change requests before taking action.

### 2. Password Reuse and Weak Authentication

Reused credentials can allow attackers to access multiple systems after one account is compromised.

**Expected behaviour:**

- Use unique passwords for every account.
- Enable multi-factor authentication where supported.
- Never share credentials.
- Follow the approved password and access-control policy.

### 3. Improper Handling of Sensitive Information

Employees may expose customer information through insecure sharing, unauthorised access, or poor physical document handling.

**Expected behaviour:**

- Share information only with authorised recipients.
- Use approved systems to store and transmit sensitive information.
- Follow data-protection and records-handling procedures.
- Report accidental disclosures immediately.

## Delivery Strategy

The programme uses affordable, accessible methods suitable for a distributed workforce and limited budget:

- Monthly 15–30-minute awareness sessions
- Short email awareness campaigns
- Practical demonstrations
- Posters and visual reminders
- Short videos, quizzes, and digital learning materials
- Mandatory security onboarding within the first seven working days
- Quarterly, controlled phishing simulations
- A confidential, simple, and no-blame incident-reporting process

Employees who interact with simulated phishing messages receive supportive educational feedback. The programme focuses on learning and safer behaviour, not punishment or embarrassment.

## Performance Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Owner | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Training completion rate** | Employees completing training ÷ employees assigned × 100 | HR Manager | Monthly |
| **Phishing click rate** | Employees clicking simulated links ÷ employees receiving simulation × 100 | Security Manager | Quarterly |
| **MFA adoption rate** | Eligible employees with MFA ÷ total eligible employees × 100 | IT Support | Monthly |
| **Incident reporting trend** | Reports during measurement period compared with baseline | Security Manager | Monthly |
| **Security knowledge score** | Correct answers ÷ total questions × 100 | HR and Security Manager | Quarterly |

An increase in reports is not automatically evidence of more incidents. It may demonstrate improved awareness, confidence, and use of approved reporting channels.

## Continuous Improvement

Quarterly reviews bring together Executive Management, Security, HR, IT Support, and department managers. The review team examines simulation results, incident reports, completion rates, employee feedback, and changes in threats.

Training content is updated when:

- New phishing or invoice-fraud techniques emerge.
- An incident reveals a knowledge or process gap.
- New systems or technologies are introduced.
- Data-protection requirements or internal procedures change.
- Employee feedback identifies accessibility or comprehension issues.

Positive security behaviour is reinforced through security-awareness recognition and responsible-reporting acknowledgement. Recognition promotes learning without publicly identifying employees who make honest mistakes.

## Limitations

- Northbridge Logistics is a fictional organisation.
- Performance targets are programme goals, not recorded company results.
- Actual rollout would require approved policies, named system owners, verified identity data, and privacy-conscious simulation procedures.
- Results may be affected by incomplete reporting, employee role changes, access differences, and limited security-team capacity.
